Understanding the Simulation & Math Metrics

🃏 The Simulation Model

This simulator deals cards from a physical shoe of cards (Fisher-Yates shuffled) without replacement. As hands are dealt, cards are popped one-by-one from the shoe and count values are tracked in real-time. When the shoe reaches the penetration threshold, it is reshuffled. This is a realistic replica of real brick-and-mortar casino conditions.

📊 Session Variance & Bell Curve (Central Limit Theorem)

Over a single 2 to 16 hour session, your total profit/loss is normally distributed. While your Expected Value (EV) grows linearly with hours ($EV \times H$), your standard deviation grows with the square root of hours ($\sigma_{\text{hr}} \times \sqrt{H}$). This is why short-term swings feel massive. The bell curve maps out your 68.3% ($\pm 1\sigma$) and 95.4% ($\pm 2\sigma$) confidence bands so you know precisely what losses to mentally prepare for.

📈 Hours to N₀ (N-Zero)

N₀ is the exact number of hands you must play for your expected value (EV) to equal one standard deviation of variance. Physically, reaching N₀ means you have an 84% chance of being in the green. The hours display shows how many casino hours (taking into account table speed/player counts) it takes to reach this threshold.

🛡️ Risk of Ruin (RoR)

The mathematical probability that you will lose your entire starting bankroll before your positive mathematical edge overcomes short-term variance. It uses the analytical formula $RoR = e^{-2 \cdot B \cdot \frac{EV}{\text{Variance}}}$ computed on round-level stats. A risk below 5.0% is considered safe for professional play.

🚪 Wonging Out (Sifting)

A professional strategy where the counter sits out when the count gets low (e.g., True Count ≤ -3). While sitting out, the dealer burns cards through mock rounds, and you place no bets, bypassing negative-EV rounds to increase your overall hourly win rate and lower volatility.

✍️ Spread Syntax

You can customize your bet spread for each True Count in the table. Enter single bets (e.g. 10 or 50) or split bets (e.g. 2x50 to play two hands of $50, which helps lower round-level variance at high advantages).
